The Interstellar Effect: Why Time is Relative

January 2025

We often think of time as a steady ticking clock, the same for everyone. But according to Einstein's Theory of Relativity, time is more like a river—it flows at different speeds depending on how fast you're moving or how much gravity is pulling on you.

This is known as Time Dilation. If you were to travel on a spaceship at 90% the speed of light for a year, you might return to Earth only to find that years, or even decades, have passed for your friends.

This isn't just a theory for astronauts; it's a reality for the GPS systems we use every day. The satellites orbiting Earth have clocks that tick slightly faster than ours because they are further away from Earth's gravity. Without correcting for this tiny difference in time, your Google Maps would be off by kilometers within a single day!
