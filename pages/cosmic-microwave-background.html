The Cosmic Microwave Background: Listening to the Beginning of Time

January 2025

If you could "see" the entire sky in microwaves instead of visible light, you would see a faint glow everywhere. This isn't light from stars or galaxies; it's the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)—the literal afterglow of the Big Bang.

Think of it as the oldest "fossil" in the universe. It dates back to about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, when the universe finally cooled down enough for light to travel freely.

By studying these ancient signals, scientists can map out the history of our cosmos. For engineers and physicists alike, it's a reminder that everything we see today—from the smallest electron to the largest galaxy—started from the same point of infinite energy. We are, quite literally, made of stardust and ancient light.
